**Rule (locked 2026-06-10):** When a storyboard panel or Seedance video clip features the hero AND adversaries in the same frame, the hero must be staged so their BODY + FACE/EYELINE is directed TOWARD THE ADVERSARIES, NOT TOWARD THE CAMERA.

The audience perspective is BEHIND/BESIDE the hero — back-three-quarter, side-profile, or over-the-shoulder — looking PAST the hero AT the threat. The hero's eyeline is the audience's eyeline. This is the fundamental cinematic principle that the hero's POV tells the audience where the action lives.

CRITICAL ADDITION FROM LYCHEE REINFORCEMENT — the back-to-camera rule applies to TRANSITION/CUE panels too, not just combat panels. If LYCHEE is looking at a distant cue (HEARTA pink heart on horizon), her BACK should be to camera with the heart visible past her ahead — NOT her face to camera with heart in BG. The audience always shares the hero's POV looking at what they see.

This breaks cinematic logic — the hero appears to be ignoring the threat in front of them to pose for the audience. Reads as cheesy hero portrait, NOT combat readiness.

**How to apply per panel type:**

| Panel type | Camera position | Hero orientation |
|---|---|---|
| **Hero entrance with adversary cluster visible** | Audience-side of hero, ~3/4 back-quarter | Hero body angled toward adversary cluster, face TURNED toward them |
| **Hero wind-up / pre-strike with adversary cluster visible** | Back-three-quarter from behind hero's shoulder | Hero's back + fist/weapon drawing back, head turned to lock onto adversary |
| **Hero combat strike connecting** | Side profile or low Dutch-tilt | Hero's body in motion vector TOWARD adversary, full extension visible |
| **Hero hero-shot (no adversaries in frame, e.g., silhouette transition)** | Any framing OK | Hero can face camera, this is the "hero portrait" beat |
| **Hero reveal silhouette (no adversaries in frame)** | Side-profile / OTS / silhouette | Hero positioning sets up the next beat |

**Critical exception**: when the panel is a pure HERO PORTRAIT or HERO REVEAL moment with NO adversaries visible (e.g., silhouette transition cue, isolated hero shot), the hero CAN face camera — that's the "this is who they are" beat. The rule only triggers when ADVERSARIES SHARE THE FRAME with the hero.
